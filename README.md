# Ashes Fall

A high-speed assault frame combat game in the browser, inspired by Armored Core, Gundam and Neon Genesis Evangelion. Build a mech in the hangar, pick an arena and a mode, and fight AI frames in free-for-all, team, conquest and king of the hill battles.

Everything runs in a single `index.html` with no build step. 3D rendering uses [three.js](https://threejs.org/) r128 from a CDN; sound effects are synthesized live with the Web Audio API.

## Play

- **GitHub Pages:** push this folder to a repository, then go to **Settings → Pages**, choose **Deploy from a branch**, select `main` and `/ (root)`, and save. The game will be live at `https://<your-username>.github.io/<repo-name>/` after a minute or two.
- **Locally:** open `index.html` in a browser. An internet connection is needed the first time to load three.js and the fonts. For the most reliable audio, serve the folder instead of double-clicking, e.g. `python3 -m http.server` and visit `http://localhost:8000`.

## Features

- **Cinematic title screen:** a randomly built and painted frame stands in the bay under the title while the camera works over it in close, slow shots: gliding down each arm gun, across the chest plate, pushing in on the head, over the shoulder weapon, past the thrusters and in a low hero arc, cutting through black. A new frame rolls in every few shots, with its designation, pilot type, parts and weapons in the corner, and the background falls out of focus behind it. The current music track is shown in the footer. Press any key to start (tap on touch-only devices).
- **The bay:** zoom in on your frame and the bay behind it softens into a depth-of-field blur. A stepped plinth with bolts, a lit lip and floodlights aimed up at the frame, floor markings, drains and oil stains, ribbed walls with pipe runs and bay signage, roof trusses with six working spotlights, a crane rail, a maintenance gantry, weapon racks, crates, carts and drums.
- **Attrition matches:** 1 to 11 opponents (up to twelve frames in an arena; solo King of the Hill takes the same range) in timed rounds of 3:30, 5:00, 7:00 or 10:00; bigger fields start spread far apart around the arena so the opening isn't a brawl. Every frame starts with a stock of lives (4 to 9, set by match length, opponent count and difficulty) and redeploys by air drop five seconds after being destroyed as a fresh, fully repaired frame, with a few seconds of protection on landing. Lives never refill, so every death counts. Run out of lives and you're eliminated; when time runs out, the most kills wins (ties go to fewest deaths, then damage). An optional kill cap (5, 10, 15 or 20; off by default) ends the match as soon as any frame reaches it. A live scoreboard, kills, lives, rank and a countdown sit on the HUD.
- **Positional sound:** every sound is placed by direction and distance from the camera.
  - **Direction and distance:** sounds to your side pan hard; nearby sounds wrap around you; sounds behind you are duller and a little quieter.
  - **Delay:** sound takes time to travel, at a stylised speed capped at about 1.25 s, so a far explosion's boom arrives after its flash.
  - **Air:** distance soaks up the treble, falling off more steeply far away, and scoops the mids, while low rumble carries.
  - **Reverb:** far sounds get a little more reverb than near ones, plus a light, long, dark "distant wash" fed only by far-off sounds; its size follows each arena's acoustics. Both sit low in the mix (`REV_MIX` = 20%).
- **Pilot profiles:** after the title screen comes the pilot registry, with three pilot slots so several people can share one machine. Each pilot keeps their own frame, saved loadouts, match setup and progression; settings are shared. A card shows the pilot's name, rank and XP toward the next rank, frame callsign, matches flown, saved loadouts and when they last flew; pilots can be renamed or deleted (click Delete twice). Click a card or press 1–3 to fly as that pilot; an empty slot creates a new pilot starting at rank 1. The Pilot button in the hangar header switches pilot. An existing save from before profiles becomes pilot 1.
- **Part lists:** opening a slot in Assembly gives its list the whole left column. The other slots in the tab fold into a strip of buttons above it (click one to switch), and the open list shows each part on one compact line (name, key stats and weight), so 14 or more parts fit at 720p and the whole armoury at 1080p. Lists with more than seven parts have a search box (by name, family or stat) and family filters (Guns, Energy, Blast, Melee, Other for weapons; Biped, Reverse, Quad, Tread, Hover, Wheel for legs). The list opens centred on the equipped part. Up/Down, Page Up/Down, Home and End move through it with a live preview, Enter equips, typing jumps to the search box, and Escape clears the search, then closes the list. The hovered part's description and full stats show under the frame readout on the right. The part summaries under the lists scroll, with a thin red scrollbar, when they don't fit the screen.
- **Saved loadouts:** the Loadouts tab in Assembly holds five complete frames per pilot (parts, weapons and finish). Save the current frame into a slot, click its name to rename it, and Load to put it back on (Undo reverts a load). Overwrite replaces a slot with the current frame, and Clear empties it (click twice to confirm). The list scrolls when it doesn't fit. The slot matching your current frame is marked as equipped.
- **Sortie in two steps:** after Assembly, the sortie takes over the whole screen in two pages, grouped under Sortie in the header, with a 1 Arena → 2 Game mode → Deploy tracker on each. **Choose your arena** lays every map out as a large card grid with a big preview, description and facts; a bar along the bottom names the selected arena and leads on with *Next · choose game mode*. **Choose the game mode** shows Free-for-all, Team Battle, Conquest and King of the Hill as four large cards, the match settings below in a fixed grid that keeps every setting (match length included) in the same place in every mode, and a deploy column on the right with the arena you're deploying to (and a Change arena link), your frame readout, the controls and the Deploy button.
- **Time of day and weather:** the arena page has a Time of day and a Weather selector for the selected arena, remembered per arena. Every outdoor map keeps its own look as the first option and adds two more times (from day, dusk, dawn and a moonlit, starry night) and two weathers (Dry Basin offers dusk, dawn and night). Rain falls on Pine Reach, Sector 9, Blackwater, Windward Hills, Cinder Works and Overgrowth, and thunderstorms roll over Dry Basin, Red Gorge, Terminus, Sector 9 and Blackwater. Iron Spine and Halcyon Shelf get snow (and a blizzard on the shelf), the desert maps get dust storms, Cinder Works gets falling ash with embers (*Falling ash*), and several maps can sink into thick fog. Weather re-lights the whole map: an overcast sky hides the sun, the haze closes in, distant ranges fade into it and water turns choppier. In rain the ground and buildings go dark and glossy, raindrops burst in small splashes on the ground around you and on the upper surfaces of every frame nearby, and you hear a rain bed. Storms add lightning bolts in the distance that light up the sky, with thunder arriving after a delay that depends on how far away the strike was. Snow, dust and ash come with gusting wind. Geofront is indoors, so it gets a Lighting choice instead: the standard work lights, Emergency (work lights out, red alarm lamps pulsing in a red half-dark) or Daylight feed (warm sunlight piped down from the surface, the light wells blazing). The selected arena's card previews the chosen conditions: the first time you pick a combination, the game renders that arena under it (a brief pause), then keeps the 36 most recent previews for later visits. The chosen conditions also show in the deploy column and on the line-up screen.
- **Game modes:** pick a mode from the four mode cards on the sortie's Game mode page.
  - **Free-for-all:** every frame for itself, 1–8 opponents, limited lives and an optional kill cap.
  - **Team Battle:** two, three or four teams, up to 12 frames in all: 2v2 to 6v6, 2v2v2 to 4v4v4, or 2v2v2v2 and 3v3v3v3. With more than two teams the bases are spread evenly around the map and each team gets its own colour: blue, red, green and gold. A small triangle in the team's colour sits beside each frame's name on its target marker and in the target camera, and stays under the marker box at long range, so you always know whose frame you're shooting at. Every enemy destroyed scores for your side. First team to the score limit (15, 30, 50 or none) wins, otherwise the team with the most kills when time runs out.
  - **Conquest:** two teams fight over control points. 2v2 and 3v3 use three points, 4v4 to 6v6 use five. Each team starts at a base at opposite ends of the map and owns the point nearest its base. Stand in a point's ring to capture it: an enemy point is neutralised first, then taken. More frames capture faster, and a contested point freezes. Holding more points than the enemy drains their tickets, and every death costs one. A team at zero loses; otherwise the team with more tickets when time runs out wins. When you're down, choose where to redeploy with the buttons or keys 1–6: your base, or any point your team holds that isn't under attack.
  - **Point placement:** each map's layout is fixed for each team size. The two halves mirror each other through the centre, so neither side gets the better ground. Each point is nudged to the best nearby spot: open, level ground to fight on, cover and a little height around it, never in water. On Blackwater the sea is the floor.
  - **King of the Hill:** one hill at a time, marked by a light beam and a very wide ring (600 m radius, its fence following the ground) so frames can spread out and duel inside it rather than piling onto one spot; AI pilots each take up their own position inside the ring. A team alone on it scores a point a second; on a contested hill, a team that outnumbers everyone else there still scores at half rate, and an even standoff scores for nobody. Every 75 seconds the hill moves to another site, always one whose ring is clear of the last one and never straight back to the one before (a warning counts down the last 10 seconds). First to the points target (60, 100 or 150) wins; otherwise the highest score at time. Play it solo (every frame for itself) or with 2, 3 or 4 teams. Redeploys take 12 seconds instead of 5, so a team that clears the hill has time to settle in before the fallen return.
  - **Team rules:** there is no friendly fire: shots, splash, fire and blasts pass through or spare teammates. Redeploys are unlimited. Allies' eyes and trim glow blue, enemies' red. Allies get light blue markers, and the radar shows allies, enemies and points. A bar at the top shows the team scores or tickets, the clock and each point's owner and capture progress. Points carry light beams you can see across the map, and a meter shows your progress while you stand in one. The scoreboard groups the teams. AI pilots in Conquest pick objectives: they attack points they don't hold, rush back to defend threatened ones, spread out across the map, and fight on the way while still pressing toward the point.
  - **XP:** team results award XP for the win, kills, damage, captures (Conquest) and time on the hill, scaled by difficulty and team size.
- **Spectate mode:** pick Spectate on the sortie screen to watch 2–9 AI frames (or two full AI teams) fight a full match on their own (no XP). A cinematic director cuts between the most interesting frames with chase, side, over-the-shoulder, fly-by, low and wide shots, and a slow orbit on every kill. Press C (or drag, or scroll) for an orbit camera: drag to swing around the frame, scroll to zoom, Tab or the arrow keys to switch frames, 1–9 to jump to a frame by rank. A bar at the bottom shows the watched frame's name, type, armour, kills, deaths and lives.
- **Console and trailer:** press `~` (the backquote key) on the title screen, in the hangar or in a match to open the console. `/trailer` shows the hidden trailer controls in the top-right of the title screen (run it again to hide them). `/unlock` unlocks every paint scheme and thruster burn; run it again to re-lock them by your rank and Survival records (anything equipped that you haven't earned goes back to the default). There you pick a song and a style; *Trailer* plays the cut and *Record trailer* records it.
  - **Songs:** *Buckrot* (the 31-second promo), *Measured March* (64 s), *Desert Watch* (71 s) and *Shadow March* (65 s). Each song was measured for tempo and beat position, and each cut uses a section of the song with a build, a drop, the action and an ending that the title lands on; the music fades out under the title.
  - **Styles:** **Action** (fast cuts landing on the song's hits, punchy word cards, whip pans and flash cuts, camera kicks, missile cams, speed ramps), **Cinematic** (long, weighty shots, deep slow motion on the drop and on kills, soft cards, dips through black, a hand-held drift and a filmic grade) and **Minimal** (a handful of held shots, no text until the title, no flash or shake, muted colour).

  Every run is a new edit: a director plans the shots from the song's structure, with shot lengths drawn from a rhythm table that tightens in the loud passages, and cuts nudged onto downbeats and drum hits. The battles are real AI fights filmed in two or three arenas with their own time of day and weather (for example a dawn build in Pine Reach, then a night storm in Sector 9), swapped under black title cards. The camera has its own shot list: reveal crane, drone aerial, long-lens telephoto, lateral dolly, side-on tracking, hero low angle, rack focus between two frames, two-shot duel, rising spiral, slow orbit, whip pan, crane, over-the-shoulder, fly-by, chase, crash zoom, top-down, dutch close-up, push-in, worm's-eye, missile cam and kill orbits. A lens pass adds real motion blur from the camera's movement (pans blur the whole frame; travel blurs the background by depth while the subject stays sharp), zoom blur on crash zooms and the drop, chromatic fringing, film grain and a per-style grade, on top of depth of field and a letterbox that slides in. *Record trailer* saves a 1080p WebM video (`ashes-fall-trailer-<song>-<style>.webm`, 720p on Low quality) with the song and the battle audio. Press Esc to stop. Recording needs a browser with MediaRecorder support (Chrome, Edge or Firefox). The audio is only captured, and the music only fades, when the game is served over http(s), not opened from disk. The lens pass needs High quality.
- **Self-destruct:** hold G for two seconds to blow your own frame. A countdown with warning pips shows while you hold it, and letting go aborts. The blast damages and staggers enemies within about 70 m (it never hurts teammates) and costs you a life like any other death. G has to be released before it can arm again, so holding it through a redeploy won't blow the new frame.
- **Missile alerts:** the MISSILE ALERT warning, its tone and the incoming-missile markers stop the moment your frame is destroyed, and missiles that were chasing a destroyed frame lose their lock, so nothing carries over into the death cam or your redeploy.
- **Pilot progression:** every match earns XP for kills, damage, placement and survival, scaled by difficulty and opponent count. Twenty ranks from Recruit to Legend, shown in the hangar header. Every part is available from the start.
- **Layered, spatial sound:** every effect is built from layers: a sharp transient crack, a low body thump, a mid-range texture and a decaying tail, plus mechanical detail like bolt and pump clacks and falling debris. A gentle saturation and limiter on the master bus add punch without clipping. Each arena has its own acoustic space: a tight steel ring in the hangar, a long cavernous tail in the dock, and open-air slap echoes off distant terrain outdoors. Distant sounds get darker, wetter and echo more.
- **Whiz-bys:** enemy rounds that pass close to your frame snap past with a doppler pitch drop, panned to the side they passed on. Spent casings land with a faint, clean brass ting on hard ground (a lower ting from heavy rifle brass, a deep clang from the SK-7's big cannon cases, and red shotgun hulls with brass heads land with a hollow thock) and slip silently under water. Bullets crack and zing, lasers zip, rockets and missiles roar by, and near-miss rails tear the air.
- **Uncluttered markers:** distant frames show only their target box; the name, pilot type, range and armour/stagger bars fade in within 800 m, on your lock target, or when a frame is staggered. Over 110 pilot callsigns, usually numbered 01–09 and occasionally up to 99.
- **Arms that aim:** each arm gun turns (within its reach) to point down its own line of fire toward where you're aiming, and shoulder weapons swivel a little after it, so shots leave the barrel straight and you can see where a frame is aiming.
- **Real terrain:** the three newest arenas are built on heightfields: frames climb walkable slopes and hug the ground on the way down, steep faces stop them like walls, and rounds, rockets, cameras, casings, debris, scorch marks and thruster dust all follow the true ground. Rock faces are textured from the side so cliffs show strata, with grass, snow, sand or ploughed earth blended in by slope and height.
- **Glints:** the sun catches exposed metal (chrome collars, rods, bolts and rings) as a frame turns, flashing a small starburst; light paint schemes shine too, dark ones don't. Bright under clear skies, faint under the hangar lamps, and absent at night, indoors and in storms.
- **Explosions:** a white flash and fireball, rolling flame that rises and cools, debris thrown on arcs, sparks, and soft, noisy smoke billows that climb and linger as a column. Shockwave rings lie on whatever was hit, flat on the ground, upright on a wall, tilted on a slope, and face you for an air burst.
- **The world reacts:** blasts leave sooty scorch marks and rounds leave chipped strike marks on the ground and on walls, fading after about 20 seconds. Anything that lands in water (rounds, rockets, even spent casings) throws up a splash, with a ring and a tall column for explosions. Thrusters fired close to the ground kick up dust tinted to the arena; over water they raise spray, and fast low passes leave a wake.
- **Barrel heat:** every shot leaves a faint wisp at the muzzle, and sustained fire builds it (to a fixed cap) into a thin trail. Kinetic guns trail grey smoke from the muzzle that follows the frame as it moves; hot energy weapons vent a light rising steam with a faint red glow at the emitter. Guns cool off after a few seconds.
- **Distinct gunfire:** every kinetic weapon has its own voice. The rifles, machine guns and shotguns share three base sounds, but each gun gets its own pitch, weight, crack, tail and action noise, and a few have a signature layer: the Longshot and Slug boom, the Longbow snaps, the twin-barrel Chaingun doubles up, Flak pops and the Triad fires tight and dry.
- **Readable damage:** hits on you sound different by type (kinetic armor crunch, energy zap, explosive thud, heavy blade clank, fire), and rounds striking any frame land with a dull, crumpling metal clank, the direction arc and a red edge glow scale with how hard you were hit, and when your frame goes down a recap shows who destroyed you, with what, their remaining armor, and your top three damage sources that life, while the camera turns to watch your killer.
- **Hangar parts, field issue:** twenty grounded, mostly mid-weight additions: Bunker, Ridgeback, Gunner and Pathfinder heads; Battalion, Redoubt, Grenadier and Harrier cores; Trooper, Howitzer, Lineman and Recon arms; Infantry, Garrison, Camel and Halftrack legs; Sentry, Warhorse, Jumpjet and Envoy boosters.
- **Lead markers:** the left-arm and shoulder lead markers are white with a dark outline, so they stay visible against bright sky and cloud. The SB-9 Thermobaric's pressure blast reaches 2.4 times as high as it does wide, catching frames hovering above the cloud, and its fireball climbs in a column. The SK-7 Shell fires with a louder report.
- **Long-range shadows:** a second, coarse shadow layer covers about 5 km of the arena around the camera. Close up the sharp shadow map is used as before; past its edge (about 550 m out) the far layer takes over with a soft blend, so mesas, towers and ridges keep their shadows out to the horizon instead of popping in as you approach. The far layer only draws the arena itself and is redrawn only after the view moves a few hundred metres or the lighting changes, so it costs very little.
- **Detail pass:** every frame part and weapon carries a second layer of grounded mechanical detail matched to its design: hydraulic rams at elbows, ankles and nozzles, hose and cable runs, louvred vents and intakes, hatches with handles, grab rails, bolted add-on plates, bearing caps and hinge knuckles, ejection ports, charging handles, rails and muzzle brakes on the guns (no magazines or spare rounds, since nothing reloads), capacitor cells, coolant lines and heat-sink fins on the energy weapons, sight boxes and tube clamps on the launchers, mounting brackets and feed chutes on the shoulder units. A few dozen parts get a slow moving piece: sweeping sensors, searchlights, periscopes and rangefinders, small cooling and intake fans, breathing vent flaps, pumping rams, a turning feed sprocket on the HMG, and road wheels that roll with the tracks. Static detail is folded into the parts it sits on before the frame's meshes are merged, so a full twelve-frame match draws only about 2% more pieces than before.
- **Hangar parts, third issue:** the LG-B Sprinter running blades are gone, replaced by the **LG-B Courser**: light running legs on broad armored boots, nearly as fast and springy but no longer pin-thin at the ankle (saved frames with Sprinter legs get Courser legs). Fifteen new frame parts, three per category, all available from the start like every other part: **Filter** (respirator face with filter canisters), **Periscope** (low turret with a tall sensor mast) and **Owl** (big lens eyes and ear tufts) heads; **Scout** (bubble-canopy pod, light, fast dash recharge), **Radiator** (glowing heat-sink grille, +10% fire rate) and **Landship** (battleship prow, sponsons and twin funnels, very hard to stagger) cores; **Falcon** (swept vanes, fastest lock), **Engineer** (cable reels and tool racks) and **Dozer** (bulldozer-blade forearms) arms; **Jackal** (armored reverse joints), **Hoplite** (ridged greaves, clawed feet) and **Behemoth** (six-wheel tracks behind a front plough, the heaviest base) legs; and **Zephyr** (twin ramjets, fastest assault boost), **Cyclone** (big ducted fan, strong lift) and **Ursa** (armored pack with belly jets, punchy dashes) boosters.
- **AI finishes:** AI pilots mostly wear service colours and field camouflage (Ash, Gunmetal, Olive Drab, Field Grey, Woodland, Desert Digital, Naval Haze and the like), about four in five frames, with the occasional loud scheme mixed in.
- **Paint schemes:** 46 finishes: 22 standard colourways, 14 field schemes (woodland, desert tricolor, urban and desert digital, arctic splinter, jungle tiger, steppe, night digital and service colours like field grey, naval haze and black ops), and 10 special schemes earned by rank, from Kodiak winter tiger at rank 2 through Red Splinter, Aggressor digital, Black Tiger, Checkmate squadron checks, Invasion Stripes, Storm Tiger, Night Raid and Ace Gold, to Legend at rank 20. Camouflage is drawn over the panel lines, and locked schemes show the rank they need.
- **Hangar:** assemble a frame from 9 slots (head, core, arms, legs, booster, two arm weapons, two shoulder units) with 152 distinct parts (intakes, pipes, chrome, circuitry and spinning turbines on every frame), 46 paint schemes, live stat previews, a weight/load limit, and a randomize button. Hover a part list and turn the scroll wheel to step through its parts without opening it (it wraps round, past NONE). A Title button returns to the start screen.
- **Blue energy weapons:** the **WE-11 TECH** is a laser submachine gun that pours out small blue bolts at 17 rounds a second at close range and never runs dry; its bolts burst in blue sparks on frames and walls, and it fires with a quieter crackle than the Pulse. The **WL-2 TANTO** is a short blue energy dagger: lighter hits than the blades, but it strikes more than twice as often and charges in half the time. Brawlers and Skirmishers carry the Tech; Brawlers and Stalkers carry the Tanto.
- **Melee assist:** your own blade lunges are easier to land. A lunge takes a partial lock (or anything in a wide cone ahead) as its target and reaches about a third further, it homes in on the target until the strike lands, and the strike connects from a little further out and from wider angles. AI pilots keep the old rules.
- **Pre-battle line-up:** every sortie opens on a spotlit stage where the camera tracks left to right along the combatants, showing each frame with a name plate (pilot type, AP, weight, legs, weapons), then pulls back to a wide shot with name tags and a VS stamp before the drop. As the camera pulls back, a solid red ASHES FALL banner slowly drops into place on the back wall. Space or a click skips it.
- **Leg types with their own handling:** bipeds, reverse joints, treads, a four-legged walker, a legless hover skirt, running blades and skate wheels.
- **Movement:** boosting, quick-boost dashes, assault boost (~750 km/h), sustained flight, and 18+ directional attitude thrusters per frame that fire based on how the mech is actually accelerating and turning (swing the mouse and the nozzles that would spin the frame puff).
- **Pilot types:** every enemy rolls a personality with its own loadout and habits. Brawlers rush in, Marksmen hang back high and snipe, Skirmishers circle and feint, Hunters chase the most damaged frame, Raptors dive from altitude and Bulwarks dig in with heavy weapons. Stalkers hold fire and creep round behind a target (preferring one already busy with someone else), strike hard at close range, then fade back out of sight. Chargers assault-boost straight through their target, unloading at point-blank on the pass, overshoot, wheel round at range and come again. Wardens bodyguard a teammate in team modes (you first, when they're on your side), placing themselves between it and its attacker and turning on whoever hits it; alone they hold their ground and always answer whoever hit them last. Each switches tactics mid-fight (pressing, flanking, holding, feinting, retreating), and their type shows on the enemy marker.
- **Soft lock:** with a full lock, every gun and launcher bends its rounds slightly toward the target. Rifles track hardest; shotguns and lobbed shells barely do. Homing missiles keep their full guidance.
- **Hover flight:** once airborne, the frame holds its height on its own. Space climbs, C descends. Stop boosting and it starts to sink, slowly at first and faster the longer you coast; any boost resets it.
- **No energy gauge, no ammo:** boost, assault boost, lift and dashes are unlimited, and every weapon fires without limit; only its cycle time holds it back. Dashes only have a short recharge. Movement is about positioning, not managing a meter.
- **Charged blades:** hold the button to charge an energy blade (the blade grows, the arm winds inward and the camera tightens; the HUD shows the charge), release to lunge. The lunge stacks on top of your current speed, so it throws you ahead of a boost or assault boost. More charge means a longer, faster lunge, up to 50% more damage than a tap and far more stagger; even a tap staggers most frames. AI pilots charge too.
- **Stagger:** every weapon has an impact value; fill a frame's stability meter and it's frozen for 2 seconds, can't fire, and takes +50% damage.
- **Locational damage:** hits land on the head, core, arms, legs or booster. Parts smoke, spark and burn as they weaken, then blow off: lose an arm and its weapon, the head halves lock range, the booster kills assault boost, legs get crippled, and a core breach destroys the frame outright.
- **Distinct projectiles:** solid-round guns fire tracers sized to the weapon (long thin marksman streaks, stubby shotgun pellets), the Pulse fires glowing red laser bolts, plasma shots pulse with a white-hot core, and the railguns (Lancer, Halberd) hit instantly along the crosshair with a lingering white beam wrapped in a red helix. Railguns get no aim assist.
- **Target cam:** while you're locked on, a small black-and-white lo-fi monitor on the right of the HUD shows a live action camera of your target: pixelated, staticky, cutting between chase, orbit and low angles, shaking and flaring as your hits land, and dropping to static with SIGNAL LOST when the frame goes down.
- **Violent deaths:** a destroyed frame goes up in a fireball with chained secondary blasts, a shockwave that shoves nearby frames, and a spray of torn plating. It comes apart: arms, head, shoulder units and booster fly off burning, then the torso tears off the legs. Frames killed in the air tumble and shed parts as they fall, then burst on impact.
- **Spent casings:** kinetic guns throw brass out of the side of the weapon that tumbles, bounces and fades after a few seconds (one instanced pool, so it stays cheap).
- **Casings by gun and round:** every gun throws its own size of case: the SMG's is small and short, the gatling, chaingun, carbine, burst rifle and rifle step up through close but distinct calibres, then the HMG, Longbow, sniper rifle and the shell cannon's long heavy case. Shotguns eject hulls sized per gun, with the Grapeshot the fattest. The loaded round marks each one the way real munitions are: armour-piercing comes in a steel case with a black band at the base, sabot in a longer, slimmer steel case with a blue band, incendiary brass carries a red band at the mouth and HE-frag a yellow one. Shotgun hulls are red for buckshot and slugs, orange for incendiary, olive for flechettes and yellow for frag slugs. Nail guns spit steel rivets with yellow tips, or black tips for AP. Steel cases ring a little duller when they land.
- **Lead reticle:** locked targets show where to put your shots for every projectile weapon, arms and shoulders, guided or not: a diamond for the first weapon, a square for the left arm and circles for shoulder weapons whose lead sits apart.
